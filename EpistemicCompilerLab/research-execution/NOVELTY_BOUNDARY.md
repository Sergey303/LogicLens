# Novelty Boundary — Compile, Don't Teach

Status: **NARROW / producer scope refresh complete; pending independent re-review**  
Work package: `WP-003 / ENG-155`  
As of: **2026-08-07**

## Decision

The search does not support `first`, `unique`, `unprecedented`, a general neuro-symbolic-method claim, a general compiler-runtime claim, a general offline-compilation claim, a decision-graph/router novelty claim, or a teacher-to-student distillation novelty claim.

The first independent review closed the original broad-claim problems only after adding SIGIL and the missing offline-compilation work. Independent re-review round 2 then found a narrower freshness problem: ENG-200/201/202 deliberately expanded the causal ladder after Q11/Q12 were frozen. The bounded Q13/Q14 refresh therefore adds router/SOP/schema-adaptation and weight-adaptation controls while keeping the scientific center fixed.

No single reviewed primary source currently matches the complete surviving fixed-weight causal design. Almost every broad architectural or transfer mechanism around it is occupied. The contribution must therefore remain a positive matched comparison of **authoritative semantic-result placement**, not the union of compiler, router, executable-program and distillation mechanisms.

## Occupied territory

### Solver-aided, program-aided and executable-program reasoning

PAL, Program of Thoughts, Faithful Chain-of-Thought, SatLM, Logic-LM, LINC, Binder/NLEP-style work and related systems already establish LM-generated programs or symbolic specifications, external execution, and gains from offloading computation or deduction. Correctness is commonly conditional on the parsed/generated specification.

The flagship may not present external deterministic reasoning, theorem proving, Prolog execution, teacher-generated Python, code execution or translation-solve-render decomposition as its invention. ENG-201 is a matched executable-program comparator, not a novelty claim.

### Typed compiler/runtime ownership and executable harnesses

**SIGIL (RW-042)** occupies especially close territory. It compiles prose skills into a typed AG-IR, marks steps as model-owned or code-owned, preserves source provenance, applies compile gates, lowers deterministically into executable harnesses, and directly compares prose versus compiled execution.

**SkillSmith (RW-038)** compiles skill packages offline into executable boundary contracts and demonstrates reuse of stronger-model compiled artifacts across smaller or more efficient runtime models and multiple harnesses.

**SkCC (RW-048), SkillRT (RW-045), Auto (RW-047), skill-program work (RW-046), and Anything2Skill (RW-049)** further occupy typed skill IRs, portable compiled artifacts, JIT/runtime solidification, executable intervention programs, verified reusable behavior and knowledge-to-skill compilation.

The flagship therefore may not claim the first compiler-runtime architecture, typed model/code ownership, prose-to-executable harness, stronger-model reusable artifact, portable compiled skill, or general `Compile, Don't Teach` paradigm.

### Formal semantics compiled into executable tools

Ontology-to-tools (RW-039) compiles formal ontological specifications into executable LLM-callable tools that enforce semantic constraints during knowledge-graph construction.

The flagship may not claim that formal knowledge or ontologies are newly made executable for LLM agents. Its remaining question is narrower: how an already computed authoritative epistemic result should be placed at the model/runtime boundary and rendered by a frozen small model.

### Offline deterministic and execution-verified artifacts

FACTS (RW-043) generates reusable SQL/Jinja2 templates offline and then executes queries/renders summaries deterministically. Skill Discovery (RW-044) uses offline simulation and execution feedback to curate reusable verified scripts for runtime automation.

The flagship may not claim novelty for generating executable artifacts offline, validating them before reuse, or reducing repeated runtime generation/interpretation.

### Decision graphs, SOP routing and symbolic routers

**SOP-Agent (RW-052)** explicitly represents a Standard Operating Procedure as a decision graph, selectively traverses that graph to guide an LLM agent, and filters the valid function set. Other decision-tree and routing work further establishes symbolic or learned routing as broad mechanism territory.

Therefore:

- a decision graph guiding an LLM is not novel;
- an SOP graph restricting available tools is not novel;
- a Prolog/rule/decision-tree router selecting a prompt, tool or action is not novel;
- ENG-200 is a matched causal comparator only.

The scientific question for ENG-200 is whether moving teacher knowledge into a routing policy changes the same frozen endpoint under fair information/schema controls—not whether symbolic routing itself is new.

### Tool-schema adaptation is an alternative explanation

**PA-Tool (RW-053)** is especially important for causal interpretation. It adapts tool-schema naming to a small model's pretrained knowledge without retraining and reports substantial tool-use gains together with a large reduction in schema-misalignment errors.

This means an apparent gain in M15/M20/M22-style tool-selection or routing conditions can arise from **schema/interface naming alignment**, even if teacher reasoning or routing contributes nothing.

The benchmark must therefore either hold tool names/descriptions/schema information equivalent across the relevant modes or include an explicit schema-adaptation control. Schema naming, identifier transparency and catalogue wording belong to the causal accounting, not implementation trivia.

### Tool use and modular systems

MRKL, ReAct, Toolformer, ART, Gorilla, ToolLLM, CRITIC and related work occupy routing, tool calls, observations and modular neural/discrete systems. Keeping a model fixed while calling an external tool is not by itself a contribution.

### Structured generation

PICARD, grammar-constrained decoding, LMQL, guided generation, SynCode, XGrammar, JSONSchemaBench and grammar-constrained logical parsing occupy syntactic/schema guarantees.

Typed JSON alone is not the contribution. Any result attributed to a computed conclusion must survive a structured-output condition that carries the same shape but withholds the authoritative conclusion.

### Prompt/program optimization and contextual teaching

APE, OPRO, ProTeGi, DSPy, Promptbreeder, EvoPrompt, Self-Refine, CRITIC and related systems occupy automatic prompt generation, optimization and iterative refinement.

The flagship may measure bounded effects and failure modes of contextual teaching, but it may not imply that prompt/program optimization is new or universally ineffective.

### Teacher-generated supervision, distillation and Qwen weight adaptation

Weight-changing transfer is established and is now an explicit boundary rather than background context.

- **Training Language Models to Use Prolog as a Tool (RW-035)** establishes small-model Prolog-tool training.
- **SmartAD (RW-054)** distills full teacher reason-act-observe trajectories into 1.5B/3B students with capacity-aligned trajectory selection and segment-weighted supervision.
- **Skill-Aware Data Selection and Fine-Tuning (RW-055)** fine-tunes Qwen3-4B/8B on selected examples from a teacher-generated corpus.
- **REDI (RW-056)** uses positive and negative teacher traces in SFT plus reinforcement distillation for Qwen-REDI-1.5B.
- **AgenticQwen (RW-057)** trains small Qwen-family agents with synthetic-data flywheels and reasoning/agentic RL for real tool use.

Therefore teacher-generated SFT, LoRA/QLoRA-style adaptation, teacher-trace distillation, reinforcement distillation, small-Qwen tool-use training and generated-workflow RL are not novelty claims for this project. **ENG-202 is a boundary experiment only.** Its purpose is to compare a weight-changing transfer destination against the fixed-weight interface ladder, not to introduce distillation.

### Distillation-induced behavioral convergence

**When Agents Look the Same (RW-058)** shows that distillation can create teacher-specific convergence in tool-use action graphs and response patterns beyond general task improvement.

For ENG-202 this is an important diagnostic warning: behavioral similarity to a teacher after adaptation does not demonstrate the fixed-weight semantic-placement mechanism. Adapted-model gains and teacher imitation must be reported as a separate causal regime.

### Readout/copying shortcuts in small models

The Readout Shortcut (RW-050) shows that fixed 1–3B instruction-tuned models can copy answer-like trailing values rather than causally use intermediate reasoning.

A conclusion-bearing compiled frame cannot count as evidence for the flagship mechanism if the renderer can succeed by copying a ready answer. Structured-no-conclusion and explicit answer-copying controls are part of the contribution boundary, not optional robustness checks.

### Layer-wise workflow diagnosis

PROTEA (RW-051) scores intermediate workflow artifacts and localizes likely faulty nodes before targeted refinement.

The flagship may not claim generic layer-wise workflow diagnosis as new. Its narrower evaluator contribution is an independently implemented oracle/scorer aligned to its specific source/query/status/policy/frame/rendering contract.

## Positive remaining contribution

Subject to independent WP-003 review and later W0 gates, the candidate contribution remains:

> A preregistered matched causal evaluation of **authoritative semantic-result placement** for fixed-weight small language models under a four-state strict epistemic contract, comparing contextual/raw, teacher-edited, structured-no-conclusion, trusted-executed, conclusion-bearing and answer-copying conditions, with an independently implemented layer-specific oracle/scorer.

It has six inseparable parts:

1. **Matched causal comparison:** the executed/result-frame mode is compared with the DEV-selected strongest matched non-compiled representation, not Raw Prolog alone.
2. **Fixed-weight boundary:** student weights and frozen student artifacts are identical across the core conditions.
3. **Strict epistemic contract:** `supported`, `refuted`, `unknown`, and `conflicting` remain distinct and carry policy/provenance/warning/evidence obligations.
4. **Authoritative-result placement:** the experiment changes where the computed semantic conclusion enters the pipeline rather than merely adding tools, a compiler, router or training recipe.
5. **Shortcut controls:** structured/no-conclusion and answer-copying conditions isolate execution from format, extra information and ready-answer copying; schema naming/alignment is also controlled where tool choice exists.
6. **Independent layer evaluation:** source, query, runtime status, policy, frame transport and rendering are scored separately without importing the production compiler as oracle.

The expanded ENG-200/201/202 ladder is a **falsification and boundary ladder around this fixed-weight core claim**. It does not enlarge the novelty claim to every transfer destination.

## Viability conditions

The central claim survives only if:

- baseline selection is frozen on DEV;
- the scenario-level clustered effect is practically meaningful;
- the direction replicates independently;
- structured/no-conclusion and answer-copying controls do not explain the result;
- schema naming/identifier visibility do not explain tool-selection/routing gains;
- no single model/domain/status/source family creates the pooled effect;
- independent oracle/scorer and mutation audits pass;
- renderer obligations remain non-trivial after authoritative execution;
- weight-changing ENG-202 results are reported as a separate boundary regime rather than folded into the fixed-weight estimand;
- the manuscript differentiates the result directly from SIGIL, SkillSmith, ontology-to-tools, FACTS, Skill Discovery, SOP-Agent, PA-Tool, LINC, Logic-LM, Faithful CoT, SatLM, Readout Shortcut, SmartAD, Skill-Aware, REDI, AgenticQwen and the newer skill compilers.

Null or negative evidence forces either a narrower failure-analysis contribution or abandonment of the flagship causal claim.

## PIVOT / STOP

**PIVOT before scaling** if a refresh finds a paper combining fixed-weight small students, contextual/raw/teacher-edited interfaces, trusted executed semantic results, typed four-state epistemic states, matched structure/copying controls and independent layer evaluation.

**STOP the flagship route** if the only favorable comparison is solver versus no solver; the result-frame mode loses to the strongest matched control; copying/ready-answer or schema-alignment conditions explain the effect; evaluator independence fails; replication reverses; or the paper requires universal/privacy/learning/priority wording to appear novel.

## Saturation boundary

Legacy Q1-Q10 remain non-reproducible saturation evidence. Q11/Q12 are reproducible and closed the original B1-B6 remediation, but they are now **superseded for final W0 coverage** because ENG-200/201/202 expanded the transfer ladder after those rounds were frozen.

The latest usable rounds are therefore **Q13 and Q14**. Q13 attacks decision-graph/SOP/symbolic routing and tool-schema adaptation. Q14 attacks teacher-generated supervision, agent distillation, Qwen SFT/RL and teacher-trace distillation. All included and excluded candidates are recorded in the existing screening ledger, every included source is scored in the seven-dimension ledger, and neither round finds a source with at least five strict `yes` values across the seven defining core dimensions or an exact match to the surviving fixed-weight positive comparison.

This supports only **provisional producer-side search saturation after the transfer-ladder refresh**. It is evidence of current search coverage, never proof of absence. Independent review remains mandatory, and the search must be refreshed before final claim freeze and immediately before submission.
